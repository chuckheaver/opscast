Home Loans Follow the Bond

Gold is your home loan, blue is what the government pays. The gap between them barely moves, so the bond leads and your rate follows.

The Yield Curve Today

Lend longer, earn more — the line rises, as it should. When it tips the other way it is inverted, which has come before every U.S. recession since the 1970s. We are not there.

Annual Opportunity Cost of Owning an $8M Home

  • Lost bond earnings (5.29%) $423,000
  • Real estate taxes (1.18%) $94,400
  • Total $517,400
What moves the financed buyer
  • The 10-year Treasury leads; home loans run about 1.7 points above it.
  • Treasury under 4.5% → loans in the low 6s → buyers come back.
  • Treasury above 5% → loans above 7% → fewer buyers qualify.
  • Watch the bond, not the Fed.
What moves the cash buyer
  • 1. Need — a family that has outgrown the house buys this year, at whatever the market asks. Outranks the other two.
  • 2. Liquidity — can they get at the money: share sales, tender offers, IPOs.
  • 3. Opportunity cost — cash left in 10-year Treasuries earns 5.29%, free of California tax.
Payments are principal and interest only. Foregone yield uses the 10-year Treasury; property tax at 1.18%. Insurance, upkeep and illiquidity are additional.page 4 / 9
