The National Picture
Four numbers set the rules for every sale in the city. None of them are decided in San Francisco.
Chuck Heaver · Vanguard Properties
Freddie Mac · U.S. Treasury · BLS · EDD

Mortgage Rates — What a Loan Costs

The rate on an ordinary 30-year loan: 6.10% in January, down to 5.98% in February, 6.76% now. A higher rate means a bigger payment for the very same house.

The 10-Year Treasury — What Sets That Rate

What the government pays to borrow for ten years. Banks price home loans off this, then add about 1.7 points. It was 4.04% a year ago and is 5.02% today — the highest since 2007.

Inflation — How Fast Everything Costs More

How much more things cost than a year ago: 2.4% in January, up to 4.2% in May, 3.4% now. While this stays high, loans stay expensive.

Jobs — Who Is Working

Share of people looking for work. San Francisco is at 3.7% against 5.1% statewide and 4.1% nationally — more people working here means more people able to buy.
How the four fit together. Inflation keeps the Treasury high. The Treasury sets the mortgage rate. The mortgage rate decides what a financed buyer can pay. Jobs decide how many buyers there are at all. Right now the first three are working against buyers and the fourth is working for them — which is why prices rose +24% in a year of expensive money.
